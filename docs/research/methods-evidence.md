## What the archive can establish

Daily individual/institutional buy/sell value and volume can describe **executed ownership transfers, participation and their distribution**. They cannot by themselves establish investor skill, price pressure, new cash entering the exchange, investment returns, participant counts, ownership holdings, market capitalization or liquidity in the transaction-cost sense. Each purchase has a seller. A positive individual balance is an equal institutional reduction within a reconciled two-category population.

The supplied brief and repository README describe mixed instruments and exact symbol strings. This report does not independently audit raw sheets; the root researcher owns that audit. Common-equity, rights, fund, debt and derivative populations must remain separate after verified classification. Until then, label sums as the **observed mixed-instrument table**, not the stock market. Keep exact symbols, source date/revision, publication timestamp, field units and independent value/volume validation. A missing row is not a zero or a nontrading session. A revision selected retrospectively may be suitable for descriptive history but introduces look-ahead in a simulated strategy unless original publication versions are available.

### Notation and availability

For instrument i on session t, let BI, SI, BJ, SJ denote individual buys/sells and institutional buys/sells, in value units. Let QBI, QSI, QBJ, QSJ be corresponding quantities. Require finite nonnegative inputs and checked identities BI+BJ=SI+SJ and QBI+QBJ=QSI+QSJ within independently justified source precision. Define one-sided traded value T=BI+BJ and quantity Q=QBI+QBJ. **BI+BJ+SI+SJ=2T−eV**, equal to 2T only when eV=0; using this as turnover silently halves normalized ratios.

Define F=BI−SI, f=F/T for T>0 and G=Σ_i |F_i|. U_t is the declared eligible observed population. All divisions require positive denominators; otherwise return unavailable with a reason. All logs below are natural logs. Metrics involving several sessions use dated session windows, not the last N available reports without disclosing gaps. Price p and return r, when mentioned, require independently verified adjusted price/total-return data; investor VWAP is not p. Currency conversion, price multipliers and instrument-specific volume units require verification.

Evidence labels: **D** = established statistical measure/accounting identity, descriptive only; **H** = proposed heuristic/application with no accessed evidence of return predictability; **E** = accessed empirical research in its stated market/sample, not validated in Iran; **B** = blocked by missing enrichment. “Now” always means conditional on checked fields and universe controls, not a claim that every workbook supports the method. Formula sources describe underlying mathematics; archive-specific adaptations are explicitly identified rather than attributed to those sources.

## Evidence boundaries and excluded shortcuts

* **Trade direction is different from owner category.** Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov examine intraday order-book events, not daily individual net purchases [S21]. An institutional buy can be passive or aggressive. These workbooks cannot reproduce order-book imbalance, Kyle lambda, effective spreads or VPIN by renaming F as order flow.
* **Retail versus institution is not uninformed versus informed.** Barber and Odean's accessed US household paper documents a performance penalty for frequent trading in its brokerage-account sample [S22]. It neither supplies Iranian participant classifications nor validates a “follow institutions” rule. Its household account-level observations are much richer than category aggregates.
* **Liquidity level versus liquidity risk:** Amihud-style |r|/T, share turnover, quoted spread and sensitivity to aggregate liquidity innovations are distinct. Pastor–Stambaugh concerns systematic liquidity risk, not any arbitrary flow/volume ratio [S14].
* **No per-capita buyer power:** BI/countBuy and SI/countSell, or their ratio, require verified count semantics, dated counts and matching populations. Number of trades is not necessarily number of people/accounts. No substitute denominator can be derived from volume/value.
* **No investor-level herding or ownership HHI:** the archive aggregates two categories. A two-category buy-share HHI is merely category balance and does not measure concentration among investors. Counts of securities with positive F are breadth, not proof of investor imitation.
* **No verified predictive result from this archive:** no return panel, executable backtest, p-value, Sharpe ratio or forecast improvement was produced in this research task. Source formulas and available-library implementations do not establish profitable trading rules.

Existing value-row checks tolerate 1 rial. Use buy-side T=BI+BJ, record eV=T−(SI+SJ), and do not treat tolerated residuals as exact conservation. A bounded intensity requires T>0 and |F|<=T; otherwise return unavailable with a reason, never clamp. Other independently checked raw metrics remain available. The first milestone uses nonzero-sign breadth (n+−n−)/(n++n−); method 7 above defines the distinct all-eligible-row variant. Names and denominator metadata must distinguish them.

## Evaluation protocol before predictive claims

1. **Audit the estimand first.** Reconcile row values and quantities separately; retain exact identifiers and source units. Declare partial-panel eligibility, date/revision selection and all inclusion counts. Test universe changes using #14. A passed numerical identity alone does not establish the table's instrument scope or complete market coverage.
2. **Preregister a small experiment.** Begin with f, breadth, concentration and one lagged-flow baseline; freeze window lengths, minimum observation/traded-value criteria, test dates and target. Current data can evaluate subsequent *flow* sign/level and reporting stability, not investment returns. Negative results are useful.
3. **Split by session date across the entire cross-section.** Keep every instrument for a date in the same fold. Use expanding or rolling training, a validation interval and an untouched later test interval. TimeSeriesSplit documents future-to-past leakage and its equal-spacing caveat [S18]; ordinary row-level random folds are unsuitable. A flat instrument-date table needs a date-grouped splitter. Fit scaling, winsorization, regressions and thresholds only within each training window.
4. **Align the information clock.** A daily report published after trading cannot fund same-close execution. Delay to the first verified feasible session; preserve initial publications and corrections. Purge training labels whose forward-return windows overlap validation/test intervals; a gap parameter does not automatically solve every overlap. Uneven or missing sessions require an explicit exchange calendar.
5. **Use honest baselines and uncertainty.** For flow forecasts report MAE or sign calibration/Brier score against historical-median/unconditional-frequency baselines. After return enrichment report cross-sectional Spearman IC, average/median IC, return quantiles and block-based confidence intervals; dependent panels require date/cluster-aware inference. ADF [S23] can diagnose a unit-root null in a sufficiently long series, but does not prove stationarity or justify trading a cumulative-flow series.
6. **Count the experiments.** Log every tried feature, horizon and universe. Apply an appropriate multiplicity procedure to valid test p-values [S6]; dependent tests require attention to correction assumptions. Benjamini–Hochberg does not erase feature selection or repeated holdout peeking. Keep exploratory and confirmatory results distinct.
7. **Prove economic implementability after enrichment.** Report gross and net performance, turnover, drawdown, concentration, capacity as a fraction of contemporaneous traded value, cash, delistings, halts and no-fill scenarios at constrained prices. Use dated instrument-specific costs and operational rules from separate market-structure research; do not invent universal fees or trading limits. A high hypothetical long-short spread is not an executable shorting strategy.

## Alternative first milestones

**A. Descriptive participation and coverage observatory (strongest fit now).** Deliver checked conservation, f, positive/negative/neutral breadth, HHI/effective breadth, top contributors and matched-panel changes. Every chart shows its population, valid rows, omissions and selected revisions. Acceptance: identities hold; four equal nonzero transfer weights yield HHI=.25, N_eff=4 and N_H=4; a single transfer weight yields both effective counts=1; all-zero transfer yields undefined transfer weights, not an invented zero concentration. Entrant/exit decomposition reproduces the full aggregate change.

**B. Anomaly and persistence research notebook/report.** Evaluate past-only MAD anomalies, adjacent-session transitions, flow autocorrelation and distribution shifts on a fixed specification. Acceptance: gaps do not extend streaks; constant windows return an explicit degenerate baseline; matched-support results disclose lost coverage; forecasting claims concern flow and are evaluated against simple baselines on later dates. Label alerts as unusual observed activity rather than buy/sell advice.

**C. Small price-enrichment pilot before adding an indicator library.** Select a historically defined, verified common-equity cohort, retain exact-symbol mappings and original reports, then acquire corporate-action-adjusted prices, publication times and trading states. Validate units and corporate actions manually on several events. Compare f and residual flow with one price-momentum baseline; record realistic delayed execution and no-fill cases. Broader factor/portfolio work follows only if the pilot's data and evaluation gates pass. A survivorship-free population still requires historical listings/delistings beyond the pilot cohort.

These alternatives have different deliverables; they are not three prerequisites that must all be implemented. A offers useful, defensible output immediately, B studies behavior without pretending to forecast returns, and C addresses the binding data constraint for investment research.
